Beating a Dead Horse

My car breaks down in mid-July because the battery gives out and oil starts to leak through the engine. I lament the $1400 repair cost to my housemate and he tells me to stop filling it up with E10. I say something about the cost of living crisis and the next hour is spent bitching about the Current State of the World, and oil reserve scarcity, and the five cents it would have cost to buy a house in Fitzroy in the 90s.

Minutes later and I’m on the tram. I’m complaining over the phone to my friend about car repair costs, and the cost of living crisis, and oil reserve scarcity, and inflation, and the moral quandary that is owning a car alongside the looming threat of environmental catastrophe when the ticket inspectors get on and I am forced to exit at the next stop.

“Fucking ticket inspectors,” she says. “Least sexy job in the world.”

I propose a pint with some of the others and she agrees under the provision that she is allowed to talk about the new myriad of Bad Things her ex has done since they got back together. I sigh, but I can’t pretend I don’t love it a little bit, and she obviously needs to keep flogging it, and if I look two hours forward, I already know that this will end in a promise to break up and the agreement that you deserve so much more than this. You really, really do.

I attempt to rally the troops, but one friend can’t come because she’s spent $48 to watch some planes go by in a Tullamarine carpark, and the other has spent the past two hours huffing perfume samples at Myer and says she feels “delirious and slightly horny. Besides, there’s no point in trying to prevent her from going back. She loves to push that damn boulder.”

I bump into ten people I know on the way to the pub and tell every single one of them about my broken car. Most are sympathetic, but this one guy who’s never even had a driver’s license asks me why I wouldn’t just sell my car because you hardly use it anyway and no one even needs a car in Melbourne. I feel my face go red hot, and my hands shake and contort in anger. Why wouldn’t I just sell my car? Are you serious? Do you even know what love is? We’ve been together for four years! I still remember our first time… Williamstown, 2022, a little awkward and nervous, tentatively pressing down the clutch as we drifted gently along the waterfront. To turn my back on that would be… wrong! Just plain wrong! An insult to our mechanic affinity!

“It just sounds like it’s maybe causing you more stress than you need.”

Well, you wouldn’t get it anyway. I’ll drive until it falls apart in my hands and then some, you blithering idiot.

At the pub now, and the table conversation shifts to employment. One friend says (again) that he hopes his work gets firebombed overnight, and we (again) tell him that he needs to quit. But he won’t, and we know he won’t, and so we switch gears to exes again and the merits of staying together, or breaking up, or staying together, or… It’s a redundant exercise, with an inevitably regressive end, and we begin to tire of problem solving. “I don’t know why I can’t just stay away,” she sobs, and we follow it with it’s just love, it’s okay, it’s okay.

The next day I twist my key in the ignition and am horrified when the engine refuses to turn over. Tears rolling down my cheeks, I press my forehead against the steering wheel and whisper please, please, please just start. Please. For me. I place the key in the ignition again. I twist. Nothing.
