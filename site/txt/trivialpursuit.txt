Trivial pursuit

In the British television show The Chase, a team of four middle-class laymen go head-to-head with one of the greatest quizzing minds in the United Kingdom. On the 10th of July 2009, this mind belonged to Mark “The Beast” Labbett, a former pub trivia star who ranked 89th in the 2012 World Quizzing Championships. Labbett attended Bishop Wordworth’s School in Salisbury, an English boys’ grammar school, and then went on to complete a Master of Arts degree in mathematics from Exeter College, Oxford. Oxford University holds a steady #4 position in the QS World University Ranking and further study for domestic residents costs upwards of 15,000 British pounds. Scholarships are sparse and highly competitive. In 2014, Labbett married his second cousin, Katie, with whom he has a son. The two divorced in 2020, the same year that the quizzer acquired an early strain of the COVID-19 virus. An insatiable sugar addict, Labbett has several false teeth and recently joined the Celebs Go Dating agency after successfully losing 63 kilograms.

On the 10th July 2009, Jackie, Malcolm, Wendy, and Chris attempt to take on Labbett and secure a variable cash prize predicated on their ability to correctly answer general knowledge questions. Like most office-cogs and retail assistants, the four want to use their prospective winnings of 21,000 pounds to go on a nice holiday and spend some more time with their families. The two-minute timer begins and Labbett answers 12 questions correctly with 47 seconds remaining. Jackie, Malcolm, Wendy, and Chris are deflated. The Beast folds his hands across his stomach and smirks from behind his unnatural pearly whites. The host congratulates him for his victory and ability to shake off both a rocky beginning and some unkind ribbing from the other contestants. Sitting smug on his elevated platform, Labbett replies “it’s not how you start, it’s how you finish.”

Average Joe is watching this episode from a treadmill at the city gymnasium. Located on the main street of the metropolitan grid, Joe has been dragging himself here three times a week for the past two months. This new routine was prescribed to him alongside 20 milligrams of escitalopram and the suggestion that he should lay off the Happy Hour pints and fast-food burritos. Now, Joe brings his meal prep to the office and permits himself one Vodka Lime Soda a week. He has lost two inches from his previous size forty-three waistband and is aiming to lose eight more. 

Joe scowls as the television cuts to commercial break. Usually he would use this time to check his phone, or relax by looking at the scenic simulation on the treadmill screen, but today he is forced to notice his hands. They are clammy and have started to shake. His mouth is dry and foul-tasting, and there is a white-hot pressure building behind his eyes. An alert on his fitness watch informs him that his heart rate is approaching a dangerously high 190 beats per minute, roughly 60 beats over the target zone for efficient fat loss and fatigue prevention. Distressed, he presses the immediate stop on the treadmill and exits the gymnasium. He arrives at the emergency department around 30 minutes later, reporting chest pain and a family history of cardiac arrest on his paternal side. He is discharged after three hours with a diagnosis of severe and acute episodic anxiety and is advised to take 10 milligrams of diazepam should his symptoms persist and interfere with his ability to work.

Joe calls an Uber and returns to his one-bedroom apartment in an inner-city suburb. He pays around $450 a week, and has an induction stovetop, no pets, and no partner. He slides a My Muscle Chef Thai chicken curry meal into the microwave and cooks it as per packet instructions. He polishes it off on the couch alongside a glass of diet creaming soda and the current season of The Block Australia. Placing the plastic on the coffee table, he lays back and tries to remember the last time he masturbated. Was it this month? Or last month? As the television switches to an advertisement break, he runs a hand across his crotch and feels no sensation. Another gulp of the zero joule drink and he decides to abandon his attempt. Maybe tomorrow. He is too pent-up today, and he needs to sleep soon. If he does not, he will miss the optimal seven to eight hour window for muscle recovery, testosterone retention, and cognitive function.

The commercial ends and the programme switches over to The Chase. Joe feels his forehead begin to perspire and nervously rummages around in his pocket for the diazepam. He swallows one with the remainder of the creaming soda and feels his eyelid twitch when the chaser is revealed to be Mark Labbett. Goddamnit, thinks Joe. That smug bastard. He realises that he hates Labbett more than anyone alive. He even hates him more than the guy from high school who started trading cryptocurrency early and now owns multiple properties. Well, old Joe thinks he is doing just fine, thank you very much. And tomorrow he will get to drink a Vodka Lime Soda at the office Happy Hour, provided he attends the gymnasium during his lunch break. God, why did he stop doing pub trivia after university? Why did he negate mathematics extension classes in favour of the casual cashier job he acquired at seventeen? He could have been something. He could have been The Chaser. 

Eyes heavy from the diazepam, he switches off the television set and retires to his bedroom. Shedding his grey sweatsuit, he closes his eyes and enters a peaceful, unanxious slumber. In 2021, Labbett was reported to have purchased a home in the affluent neighborhood of Rotherham, South Yorkshire. He has a spacious kitchen, and shares it with his golden retriever, Baloo, and his son on alternating weekends. Over Instagram, Labbett showed fans his bedroom, which features minimal decor and white furnishings. As of 2026, he boasts an estimated net worth of 12.3 million pounds and remains a prominent figure on the iTV network.
